Teaching

Engineering education that moves students from watching to doing: simulations, design-build-test projects, industry problems and service in the community.

Teaching portfolio, 2025 pp. 8–254

Philosophy

I believe engineering education should empower students to think critically, solve complex problems, and innovate responsibly. Teaching extends beyond the transfer of technical knowledge; it is a responsibility to shape engineers who think systemically, act ethically, and design sustainable solutions for society. My work focuses on closing the 'Knowing-Doing Gap', moving students from passive observers to active participants through simulation, design-build-test projects, industry problems and community service. I analyse assessment data every semester to keep improving, because teaching excellence is not a destination but a continuous journey. My goal is to bridge knowledge, practice, and responsibility, inspiring students to become competent, conscientious, and innovative engineers.

Teaching portfolio, 2025 p. 8, p. 10, p. 67, p. 205, p. 253, p. 254

Visible Engineering

Her framework, Visible Engineering: Bridging Principles, Practices and Responsibilities, rests on five pillars.

  1. 1

    Scenario-Based Learning

    Immersive ethical crisis simulations. In Engineers & Society, students act as consulting engineers in an industrial crisis modelled on the Putra Heights pipeline incident, facing staged data, stakeholder pressure and unexpected twists.

    Traditional methods often leave students as passive observers who study ethics and theory but never practice them in conditions that approximate reality.

    Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 67
  2. 2

    Service Learning (SULAM)

    Community sustainability projects with schools and Majlis Bandaraya Subang Jaya. Student teams research, prototype, build education modules and run outreach, applying engineering to real community needs.

    It is not merely a volunteer exercise; it is an active training ground where students serve the community while rigorously honing their professional competencies.

    Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 115
  3. 3

    Active Prototyping (Design-Build-Test)

    Students design a heat exchanger, model it in AutoCAD and Excel, then build a small prototype from recycled materials and justify their material choices.

    This outcome-based framework forces students to step away from idealized textbook scenarios and physically construct what they have mathematically modeled.

    Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 116
  4. 4

    Industry-Embedded Project-Oriented PBL

    Unit Operations runs weekly theory alongside industry technical sharing with real plant data, assessed through reflections, technical reports, industry mentor evaluation and a boardroom pitch.

    This transforms the curriculum from purely theoretical delivery into an integrated experience where core principles are continuously anchored in industrial practice.

    Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 126
  5. 5

    Simulation-First Flipped Classroom

    Students explore simulators (Aspen HYSYS, MATLAB/Simulink, HTRI) before class, work the theory backwards from what they saw, then check predictions against pilot plant data.

    The "Simulation-First" Flipped Classroom successfully bridges the theoretical-practical divide.

    Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 171

Methods and innovations

Scenario-Based Learning (ethical crisis simulation)
Role-play of a live industrial crisis with escalating information, stakeholder pressure and technical checks, moving ethics from theory to practice.
In practice. ENG 3104 'The Industrial Crisis': teams as Lead Engineer, Communications Lead, Technical Lead and Community Liaison handle a gas pipeline leak modelled on Putra Heights.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 72
SULAM service learning
Community projects that make students apply engineering to real local needs and explain it to non-experts.
In practice. 'ChemE TrashSmart' (ENG 3104, Sem 2 2024/2025) with MBSJ and Serdang schools, funded RM1,000.00 by SULAM@UPM.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 96
Design-Build-Test prototyping
Scaffolded path from first principles to digital modelling to a physical build, with sustainability justification of materials.
In practice. Design a 2-shell-pass, 12-tube-pass heat exchanger, draw it in AutoCAD, model it in Excel and build a small prototype from recycled bottles.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 117
Industry-embedded POPBL
Weekly theory runs alongside 1-2 industry technical sharing sessions or visits per semester using real plant problems.
In practice. Unit Operations sessions such as 'CO2 capture in LNG production' (PETRONAS) and 'Membrane Fouling in Water Treatment' (Indah Water Konsortium).
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 128
Simulation-first flipped classroom
Students explore simplified simulators before class, then derive theory backwards in class and validate against pilot plant data.
In practice. Stage 1 in Heat and Mass Transfer: simulate heat exchanger performance under varying flow rates and optimise shell-and-tube area.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 158
Authentic multi-stage PBL
Place-based Malaysian waste cases with open-ended choices and built-in dilemmas; students must state and justify every assumption.
In practice. Anaerobic digestion design for 20 tonnes/day of organic waste at 70% moisture, choosing wet or dry AD.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 135
Authentic assessment
Boardroom pitches, industry mentor evaluation and technical reports replace part of traditional exams.
In practice. Unit Operations: reflections 10%, technical report 20%, industry mentor evaluation 10%, boardroom pitch 10%.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 132
Peer-Led Team Learning
Trained senior students who earned an A lead weekly 1-hour problem sessions for groups of 8-10.
In practice. Peer leaders report common difficulties back to the instructor so lectures can respond.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 172
Digital interactive tools
Interactive quizzes, games, videos and H5P modules for class and community outreach.
In practice. Kahoot! eco-enzyme quiz, Educaplay plastics quiz, Padlet project logbooks and QR-linked demo videos.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 89
Data-driven CQI
Course Assessment Summaries and Marks are analysed each semester and teaching is adjusted where outcomes fall short.
In practice. ECH 3125: EAC1 attainment 43.48% in 2022/2023 led to a redesign; reported 98.15% in 2024/2025.
Teaching portfolio, 2025, p. 254
